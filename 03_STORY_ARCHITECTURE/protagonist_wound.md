# Protagonist Wound

At the deepest psychological level, the protagonist has unconsciously built his entire identity around achievement, growth, and constant self-improvement.

Over many years, his sense of self-worth has become increasingly dependent on external progress.

Achievement is no longer something he pursues.

It has become the foundation of how he defines his own value.

He has gradually internalized a powerful set of unconscious beliefs.

* If I stop progressing, I am falling behind.
* If I am not improving, I am losing value.
* If I become average, I have failed.
* If I stop performing, I lose part of who I am.

As a result, stillness has become deeply uncomfortable.

Rest feels unproductive.

Presence feels unfamiliar.

Doing has gradually replaced being.

The protagonist does not consciously realize that his endless pursuit of growth has quietly become a psychological dependency.

He believes he is building a better life.

In reality, he has slowly built an identity that can only survive through constant progress.

The tragedy of the character is not that he is failing.

The tragedy is that he has become exceptionally successful while unknowingly constructing a life that has moved him further away from himself.

His deepest wound is not burnout.

His deepest wound is the unconscious belief that his value as a human being depends on constant achievement.
